What this repository does

It reads the speed of light as a power tower, digit by digit. Every digit is a prime raised to a power, so the number splits into three layers: the primes, their exponents, and the top. The layers rebuild the number exactly.

The rule: 4 = 2², 8 = 2³, 9 = 3²; a prime is itself to the power 1; 6 = 2·3 keeps both primes at power 1; 0 and 1 carry no prime. The cap holds every exponent at 2 or less. Change the cap or the unit.

    The Order of Waves

    The 5 → 7 and 7 → 5 layers share Ω and the same first derivative. Under a small sinusoidal drive their half-difference starts at quadratic order. Subtract its mean to see a leading oscillation at twice the drive frequency. Reversing the layers reverses its sign.

    From the original thread

    The project began with a question about patterns that survive a change of representation. These are the steps that led from the first number-theory experiments to the Prime Forms screens.

    1. Start with a digit and ask what survives

      In the m/s spelling 299792458, counting prime factors with multiplicity gives 122121213. Capping each height at two gives 122121212. The whole difference is the third factor of 2 in the final digit, 8 = 2³. That became the gold layer. Changing units re-encodes the number, which gave us a control for the mirror patterns.

      Open the unit tree · Open the phase slots

    2. Use the arithmetic as a control

      Our Goldbach picture used a centre m and a half-gap d. Goldbach varies d; the twin-prime slice fixes d = 1. At centres m = 2k, k ≥ 1, an admissible pair with both primes above 3 requires d ≡ 3 (mod 6). The twin slice hits this spine at 3 + 5, where the prime 3 is the exception. The Collatz question furnished a second exact check. For the shortcut map, the first j steps from 2k − 1 give 3j2k−j − 1 for 0 ≤ j ≤ k. These are finite identities from the discussion.

    3. Get the quarter-turn from a logistic orbit

      At r = 1 + √6, the logistic two-cycle has multiplier −1. Following a tangent perturbation on its two phases gives a matrix whose square is −I. In an adapted basis, reading the second coordinate gives 0,+1,0,−1, the values of χ₄. On the Gaussian CM curve y² = x³ − x, the negative sign selects the odd primes with vanishing Frobenius trace. The even steps are zeros of the sequence itself.

      Open the logistic fork · Logistic bifurcations · CM curve notes

    4. Put Catalan at the centre

      Use w = (s − 2)/(s + 1) to put the critical line on the rim. The Dirichlet beta function evaluated at the centre gives Catalan’s constant; at w = −½ it gives π/4. Damping the quarter-turn sequence by exp(−nt) gives the kernel 1/(2 cosh t). The weighted Gauss inverse branches give β(s) when applied once to the constant function and evaluated at zero. Green–Tao’s relative-density result lets us find arbitrarily long prime progressions within either odd remainder class modulo 4. For example, 3, 7, 11 contribute with the same negative sign to Catalan’s series.

      Dirichlet beta · Green–Tao

    5. Reverse two layers and listen for the difference

      The odd/even studies compare what changes sign under a swap with what survives the swap. The Ω study uses a separate exponential rule, Tₚ(x) = Ω p^(Ω−x), with Ω = W(1). The two orders 5 → 7 and 7 → 5 share a fixed point and a slope. Their half-difference starts at quadratic order, so a small sinusoidal drive leaves a leading second harmonic after its mean is removed. That is the amber trace in the signed plate. A separate gain control models the effect of changing a calibrated parameter in every stage.
